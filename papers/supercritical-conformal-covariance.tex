\begin{abstract}
Let $\xi>\xi_{\crit}$, and let $Q=Q(\xi)$ be the LFPP distance
exponent. We prove that the supercritical Liouville quantum gravity
metric respects the coordinate change
$h\mapsto h\circ\phi^{-1}+Q\log|(\phi^{-1})'|$ under a fixed
conformal map $\phi$. The result holds for a zero-boundary Gaussian
free field plus a random continuous function on any connected
Greenian domain, and the equality is simultaneous in all endpoint
pairs, including singular points. The main difficulty is that the
metric is not continuous in the Euclidean topology. We overcome it
by joining recovery paths to their prescribed endpoints through
short surrounding loops. A local comparison along geodesics then
improves a deterministic bi-Lipschitz bound to equality.
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}
\subsection{Overview}
A Liouville quantum gravity surface is described by a domain together
with a random generalized function. Different pairs can describe the
same surface: if $\phi:U\to V$ is conformal, the coordinate change is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:field-change}
 h^\phi=h\circ\phi^{-1}+Q\log|(\phi^{-1})'|.
\end{equation}
A metric associated with this surface should therefore give the same
distance before and after changing coordinates. The purpose of this
paper is to establish this property for the supercritical LQG metric.

Throughout we fix $\xi>\xi_{\crit}$ and write $Q=Q(\xi)\in(0,2)$.
We use one normalization of the strong whole-plane metric constructed
and characterized in \cite{DG}. Unlike the subcritical metric, this
metric has a dense set of singular points, each at infinite distance
from every other point \cite{Pfeffer}. Distances are lower
semicontinuous as functions of their Euclidean endpoints, but they
are not Euclidean continuous. Consequently, approximating a
conformal map by its derivative does not immediately give a
comparison at fixed endpoints.

Our argument follows the comparison strategy of \cite{GM}. At small
scales a conformal map becomes linear, and the transformed field
approaches the corresponding affine transform of the original field.
In the supercritical setting the resulting metric convergence allows
the endpoints of a recovering path to move. We show that this is
enough when the prescribed endpoints lie on suitable geodesic
segments: short loops near those endpoints provide connectors whose
length tends to zero. A covering by annuli containing such segments
then yields exact equality of the two metrics.

\subsection{The metric on a domain and the main result}
We first specify the metric to which the coordinate-change formula
applies. A domain is a nonempty connected open subset of $\C$.
It is called Greenian when it admits a Green function, so that its
zero-boundary GFF is defined. No regularity of the boundary is assumed.
If $D$ is an extended metric, $D(x,y;W)$ denotes its internal distance
in $W$, obtained by infimizing the $D$-lengths of paths in $W$.

\begin{definition}[The whole-plane metric]\label{def:whole}
We fix an assignment $h\mapsto D_h$ satisfying the strong LQG metric
axioms with parameters $\xi,Q$. For a whole-plane GFF plus a
continuous function, $D_h$ is almost surely a lower-semicontinuous
extended length metric. Its internal metric on an open set $W$ is
determined by $h|_W$. For every continuous $f$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:weyl}
 D_{h+f}=e^{\xi f}\cdot D_h,
 \qquad
 (e^{\xi f}\cdot D)(x,y)
 =\inf_{P:x\to y}\int_0^{\len(P;D)}e^{\xi f(P(t))}\dd t,
\end{equation}
where paths are parametrized by $D$-length. It also satisfies
translation and dilation covariance. In particular, for each fixed
complex affine map $\psi(z)=az+b$, $a\ne0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:affine}
 D_{h\circ\psi+Q\log|a|}(z,w)=D_h(az+b,aw+b).
\end{equation}
The complex affine form includes rotation invariance, which follows
from uniqueness; see \cite[Proposition 1.9]{DG}.
\end{definition}

The existence and uniqueness theorem of \cite{DG} says that this
assignment is determined up to a deterministic multiplicative
constant. We choose this constant once, for all fields and domains.
All equalities of metrics below mean equality at every pair of points
on a single probability-one event. Continuous Weyl scaling is used
on its simultaneous event, and therefore also applies to random
continuous functions which depend on the field.

\begin{definition}[The metric on a Greenian domain]\label{def:domain}
Let $h=h_0^U+f$ on a connected Greenian domain $U$, where $h_0^U$
is a zero-boundary GFF and $f$ is a measurable random continuous
function. Realize $h$, if necessary on an enlarged probability
space, as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:realization}
                  h=H|_U+F,
\end{equation}
where $H$ is a whole-plane GFF plus a continuous function and
$F\in C(U,\R)$. Set
\begin{equation}\label{eq:domain-metric}
 D_h^U(x,y)=\inf_{P:x\to y,\ P\subset U}
          \int_0^{\len(P;D_H)}e^{\xi F(P(t))}\dd t.
\end{equation}
The infimum of an empty family is $+\infty$, and constant paths
have length zero.
\end{definition}

Section~\ref{sec:domain} proves that the realization exists, that
\eqref{eq:domain-metric} does not depend on its choice, and that the
metric is a measurable function of $h$. Thus it is defined on the
original probability space of $h$. The function $f$ may depend on
$h_0^U$ and may be unbounded near $\partial U$.

\begin{theorem}[Conformal covariance]\label{thm:main}
Let $U$ be a connected Greenian domain, let $\phi:U\to V$ be a
deterministic conformal bijection, and let $h$ be a zero-boundary
GFF on $U$ plus a random continuous function. Almost surely,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:main}
 D_h^U(z,w)=
 D_{h\circ\phi^{-1}+Q\log|(\phi^{-1})'|}^{V}
                  (\phi(z),\phi(w)),\qquad z,w\in U.
\end{equation}
Both metrics are measurable functions of their respective fields
and are defined on the original probability space of $h$.
\end{theorem}

In particular, the metric respects the equivalence relation
\eqref{eq:field-change}. Formula~\eqref{eq:main} includes singular
endpoints and infinite distances. Its multiplicative constant is
exactly one, since both sides use the same whole-plane normalization.
The conformal map is fixed before the probability-one event is chosen.

For precision, the measurable functions in the theorem are chosen
for the law of the field under consideration. The construction has
the following useful countable form.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:countable}
Fix $U,V$. For any prescribed countable family of fields $h_i$ as in
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and conformal maps $\phi_i:U\to V$, one can
choose the same two measurable functions
\[
 \Phi_U:\Dscr(U)\longrightarrow[0,\infty]^{U\times U},
 \qquad
 \Phi_V:\Dscr(V)\longrightarrow[0,\infty]^{V\times V}
\]
to define all the source and target metrics. For each $i$, formula
\eqref{eq:main} holds almost surely on the probability space of $h_i$.
On every extension carrying a realization of either field, these
functions agree almost surely with \eqref{eq:domain-metric}.
\end{corollary}

The countable family is specified before the functions are chosen.
Neither statement requires one version valid for all field laws,
or a common event for all conformal maps.

\subsection{Outline and notation}
For most of the proof, $h$ is a whole-plane GFF restricted to $U$.
We compare
\begin{equation}\label{eq:d-e}
 d(z,w)=D_h(z,w;U),\qquad
 e(z,w)=D_{h^\phi}^{V}(\phi(z),\phi(w)).
\end{equation}
Section~\ref{sec:rough} proves a deterministic bi-Lipschitz bound
between $d$ and $e$. Section~\ref{sec:tangent} identifies their
common limit after zooming in around a point. In
Section~\ref{sec:endpoints} we use short loops to recover prescribed
endpoints and obtain a comparison with factor $1+\eta$ along regular
geodesic segments. Sections~\ref{sec:annuli} and~\ref{sec:finish}
turn this local comparison into $e\le d$. Applying the same argument
to $\phi^{-1}$ gives equality.

We use the established whole-plane LQG metric theory, including
annular estimates and the GFF Markov property. We state the precise
estimates when they enter. The conformal comparison, endpoint
recovery, and improvement to constant one are proved below; no
conformal-covariance theorem is assumed.

We write $B_r(z)$ for a Euclidean ball and
$A_{r,R}(z)=B_R(z)\setminus\overline B_r(z)$. Distances
\emph{across} an annulus are infima over crossing paths, and
distances \emph{around} it are infima over loops separating its
boundary components. Boundary endpoints are allowed. Whenever an
internal metric has boundary endpoints, its carrier is the closed
annulus. The notation $K\Subset U$ means that $K$ is compact and
contained in $U$. Finally,
\[
                   m_{z,r}=r^{\xi Q}e^{\xi h_r(z)},
\]
where $h_r(z)$ is the circle average of the whole-plane field.

\section{Domain metrics and a first comparison}
\subsection{Constructing the metric on a domain}\label{sec:domain}
We record the details behind Definition~\ref{def:domain}. They will
also let us work with local fields without imposing boundary
conditions on a continuous correction.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:domain}
The construction in Definition~\ref{def:domain} is well defined.
For each prescribed law of $h$, it has a measurable version depending
only on $h$. It satisfies locality, the length-space property,
restriction to internal metrics, and continuous Weyl scaling.
These assertions also hold with one choice of measurable versions
for any prescribed countable family of laws.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
First consider a disk $B\Subset B'\Subset U$. The Markov
decomposition writes the field on $B'$ as a zero-boundary GFF plus
a continuous function. Couple this zero-boundary part to that of a
whole-plane GFF $H$. The difference between the two fields on $B'$
is continuous. Multiplying it by a cutoff which is one near
$\overline B$ gives a continuous function on $\C$, and hence
defines a metric on $B$ by whole-plane Weyl scaling and locality.

We explain why these local choices agree. Suppose two whole-plane
fields plus continuous functions agree on an open neighborhood $W$.
Choose between the two fields by an independent fair coin. This
mixture is again a whole-plane GFF plus a random continuous function,
after absorbing normalization constants into that function. Locality
for the mixture gives one measurable function of the restriction to
$W$ for its internal metric. Since each component has positive
probability, this same function represents the internal metric in
both components. Thus the internal metrics agree on the original
coupling whenever the fields agree on $W$. Applying this observation
to the cutoff fields proves consistency on overlaps.

Choose a countable relatively compact cover by disks. The consistent
local metrics assign lengths to paths, and subdivision makes this
assignment independent of the cover. Infimizing these lengths gives
\eqref{eq:domain-metric}. Every path has compact image in $U$, so
only finitely many disks are needed for any given path. This also
proves the internal restriction identity and Weyl scaling. An
unbounded continuous correction causes no problem, because it is
bounded on each such compact image.

A realization on the full domain exists as well. Take a reference
whole-plane Markov decomposition
$H|_U=Z+\mathfrak h$, where $Z$ has the zero-boundary GFF law and
$\mathfrak h$ is harmonic on $U$. Sample $H$ conditionally on
$Z=h_0^U$, on a probability-space extension. Then
$h=H|_U+(f-\mathfrak h)$ there. This conditional sampling is over
the standard Borel space of distributions; it does not require the
original probability space to be standard Borel. Repeating the
overlap argument shows that any two realizations give the same
metric on a common extension.

For measurability, exhaust $U$ by compact sets $K_n$ with
$K_n\subset\operatorname{int}K_{n+1}$. The internal metric on each
$K_n$ is lower semicontinuous, by Lemma~\ref{lem:compact} below,
and is determined by the field on a neighborhood of $K_n$.
Its epigraph is a random closed set, so it has a countable measurable
description. Locality and measurable factorization give a function
of $h$ for each of these compact internal metrics. Their increasing
union determines all path lengths and hence the domain distance:
every path lies in some $K_n$, and the distance is the infimum of
the corresponding compact internal distances. This proves the
assertion simultaneously in all endpoints.

For countably many laws, apply this construction to a mixture
$\sum_i w_i\mu_i$, where all $w_i>0$ and $\sum_iw_i=1$.
The almost-sure identities under the mixture hold under every
component. The same construction therefore supplies one function
for all the specified laws. Further details concerning extensions
and descent to the original space are given in
Appendix~\ref{app:versions}.
\end{proof}

The transformed field $h^\phi$ belongs to the same class: conformal
invariance sends $h_0^U$ to a zero-boundary GFF on $V$, and
$f\circ\phi^{-1}+Q\log|(\phi^{-1})'|$ is continuous on $V$.
In particular, the target metric in Theorem~\ref{thm:main} is
defined without assuming the conclusion of that theorem.

\subsection{Compactness and annular estimates}
We use the following consequences of the whole-plane metric theory:
the local H\"older and geodesic properties in
\cite[Propositions 1.10 and 1.12]{Pfeffer}, the escape estimate in
\cite[Lemma 3.12]{Pfeffer}, and the annular estimates in
\cite[Section 2.5]{DG}. Finite-length metric paths are Euclidean
continuous. On each compact set, the identity from the metric to
the Euclidean topology has a H\"older modulus. Nonsingular points
can be joined by whole-plane geodesics. The distance between
disjoint Euclidean compact sets is positive. For any fixed
nondegenerate round annulus $A$, the random variables
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ann-tight}
 m_{z,r}^{-1}D_h(\mathrm{around}\,(z+rA)),\qquad
 m_{z,r}D_h(\mathrm{across}\,(z+rA))^{-1}
\end{equation}
are tight, uniformly in $z,r$; both annular distances are almost
surely positive and finite. These statements concern finite-length
paths and annular distances, and do not assert a finite diameter
for a Euclidean ball.

We also use the annular iteration estimate
\cite[Lemma 2.1]{DG}. In the form needed here, events measurable
from the GFF modulo constants on concentric annuli with a fixed
geometric separation have an exponentially small probability of
too few successes. If their probabilities are sufficiently close
to one, the exponential rate can be made arbitrarily large. If
their probabilities have a fixed positive lower bound, there is
some positive exponential rate. Enlarging each annulus by a fixed
collar only changes the separation factor. This estimate applies
to measurable versions of events in the completed field sigma
algebra.

\begin{lemma}[Compact path limits]\label{lem:compact}
Let $K$ be a Euclidean compact subset of a domain carrying one of
the metrics above. Paths in $K$ with bounded metric length,
parametrized at constant metric speed on $[0,1]$, have a
Euclidean-uniformly convergent subsequence. If $P_n\to P$ uniformly,
then
\[
                  \len(P;D)\le\liminf_n\len(P_n;D).
\]
The distance internal to $K$ is Euclidean lower semicontinuous.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Cover $K$ by finitely many disks where the field differs continuously
from a whole-plane GFF. Cutoff extensions and Weyl scaling transfer
the local metric-to-Euclidean modulus to each disk. A smaller
concentric cover and the positive cost of leaving its larger disks
give a common Euclidean modulus for the paths. Arzel\`a--Ascoli
therefore applies.

For a fixed partition $0=t_0<\cdots<t_k=1$, lower semicontinuity
of the local distances bounds the sum of the limiting increments
by the liminf of the lengths. Subdivide so that each piece lies in
a coordinate disk, then take the supremum over partitions.
The limit has finite metric length when the liminf is finite;
the same increment bound also makes it a metric-continuous path
after length parametrization. Finally, apply the argument to
near-minimizing paths with convergent endpoints in $K$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{A finite comparison constant}\label{sec:rough}
Keep the notation $d,e$ from \eqref{eq:d-e}, with $h$ a whole-plane
GFF. We begin with a bound which need not be close to one.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:bilip}
There is a deterministic $C\ge1$, depending only on the metric
parameter, such that almost surely
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bilip}
              C^{-1}d(z,w)\le e(z,w)\le Cd(z,w),
                         \qquad z,w\in U.
\end{equation}
The same constant compares path lengths and distances with paths
restricted to any common carrier.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix $K\Subset U$ and work on a compact neighborhood of $K$.
Uniformly for $z$ in that neighborhood, the maps
\[
 F_{z,r}(w)=\frac{\phi(z+rw)-\phi(z)}{r\phi'(z)}
\]
converge to the identity in $C^\infty$ on fixed disks as $r\to0$.
The field comparison proved in Lemma~\ref{lem:field} below, together
with affine covariance and Weyl scaling, transfers
\eqref{eq:ann-tight} to the pullback metric $e$. To see this without
using any metric covariance under $\phi$, sandwich the image of
each source annulus between round target annuli with slightly
smaller and larger radii. A loop in the smaller target annulus
pulls back to a source separating loop, and a source crossing must
cross an appropriate target subannulus. The deterministic
distortions tend to zero. A smooth field normalization differs
from the circle normalization by a tight Gaussian constant.
Thus both normalized target quantities are tight as well.

It follows that, for every $p<1$, a deterministic $C_0<\infty$
can be chosen so that for all small $r$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loop-cross}
 \Pp\left[e(\mathrm{around}\,A_{r,2r}(z))
       < C_0D_h(\mathrm{across}\,A_{r/2,r}(z))\right]\ge p.
\end{equation}
There is an analogous estimate with the metrics interchanged.
The constants are uniform on normalized univalent maps on disks
whose radii tend to infinity, hence $C_0$ does not depend on
$K,U,\phi$. The scale below which the estimate holds may depend
on them. The events are local to a fixed annular collar and
unchanged by adding a constant to $h$.

Apply annular iteration at radii between $\eps^2$ and $\eps$,
at lattice points of mesh $\eps^2/100$. Choosing $p$ sufficiently
close to one makes the failure probability at a lattice point
$O(\eps^{10})$. There are $O_K(\eps^{-4})$ relevant points.
With probability $1-O_K(\eps^6)$, every point of $K$ lies in
$B_{r/2}(z)$ for a ball satisfying \eqref{eq:loop-cross}.

We give the deterministic chaining argument, since successive
surrounding loops need not intersect. Let $P$ be a finite
$d$-length path. Use a target metric confined to a fixed compact
neighborhood of $P$, so that it is lower semicontinuous. At time
$t$, choose a good ball containing $P(t)$ in its inner half and
a target loop $\ell$ surrounding its inner ball. Its target
diameter is at most $C_0c$, where $c$ is the source crossing cost
from radius $r/2$ to $r$.

For a compact connected set $L$, let $\operatorname{Fill}(L)$ be
$L$ together with its bounded complementary components. Maintain a
reached set $R$ with the following properties: every point of $R$
is reachable from some point within $4\eps$ of $P(0)$ at cost at
most $C_0\len(P|_{[0,t]};d)$; and every separating loop whose fill
contains $P(t)$ either meets $R$ or also surrounds $P(0)$.
Initially take $R=\varnothing$.

If $P$ subsequently meets $\ell$, let $t'$ be its first such time.
It has paid at least $c$ between $t$ and $t'$. If $\ell$ meets $R$,
its entire trace is now reachable by paying its diameter. If it
surrounds $P(0)$, any point of $\ell$ is within $4\eps$ of $P(0)$
and can serve as a new starting point. Adjoin $\ell$ to $R$.
The fill property persists: a new separating loop containing
$P(t')$ either meets $\ell$, or contains all of $\ell$ in its fill;
in the latter case apply the previous fill property. The source
costs are charged to consecutive disjoint time intervals.

If $P$ never meets the chosen loop again, its terminal point lies
in that loop's fill. An already reached intersection point with
the loop is within $4\eps$ of the terminal point and can be used
as the final endpoint. If the loop also surrounds $P(0)$, the same
point can be used at both ends. No unpaid traversal of a terminal
loop is needed. The procedure terminates: at a fixed covering
scale only finitely many balls are available, and their positive
crossing costs have a positive minimum. We have obtained
$x_\eps\to P(0)$ and $y_\eps\to P(1)$ with
\[
               e_K(x_\eps,y_\eps)\le C_0\len(P;d),
\]
where $e_K$ is the confined target metric.

Choose a sequence of cover scales with summable failure
probabilities and use Lemma~\ref{lem:compact} to pass to the exact
endpoints. A countable compact exhaustion makes the argument
simultaneous for every finite-length path. Infimizing gives
$e\le C_0d$. The reverse loop-to-crossing estimate gives
$d\le C_0e$ by the same argument. Comparing the sums over every
partition of a path proves the length comparison, and infimizing
those lengths proves the last assertion.
\end{proof}

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
\section{Comparison near a linear map}\label{sec:tangent}
We next identify the metric seen after a small conformal change
of coordinates. This part of the proof concerns convergence of
distance functions, and makes no assertion about convergence at
prescribed endpoints.

\subsection{A field comparison}
Fix a smooth radial function $\rho\ge0$ supported in the unit disk
with $\int\rho=1$. Normalize the whole-plane GFF by $(h,\rho)=0$.
For $a\in\C\setminus\{0\}$ put
\[
 \rho_a(w)=|a|^{-2}\rho(w/a),\qquad h^a(w)=h(a^{-1}w).
\]
Thus $(h^a,\rho_a)=0$.
For probabilistic convergence arguments we realize the fields in
$H^{-s}_{\mathrm{loc}}$, with any fixed $s>0$. GFFs and their
continuous perturbations belong locally to this space almost
surely. Its usual countable exhaustion gives a Polish space.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:field}
Suppose $U_n$ eventually contains every compact subset of $\C$,
and $\psi_n:U_n\to V_n$ are conformal bijections with
$\psi_n(0)=0$ and $\psi_n'(0)\to a\ne0$. On every compact set,
\[
 \psi_n\longrightarrow a\,\mathrm{id},\qquad
 \psi_n^{-1}\longrightarrow a^{-1}\mathrm{id}
\]
in $C^\infty$. Moreover,
\begin{align}
 h\circ\psi_n^{-1}&\longrightarrow h^a
             &&\text{almost surely as distributions},\label{eq:field-as}\\
 h\circ\psi_n^{-1}-(h\circ\psi_n^{-1},\rho_a)
       &\longrightarrow h^a
             &&\text{locally in total variation of laws}.
             \label{eq:field-tv}
\end{align}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Apply the distortion theorem on disks $B_R\subset U_n$, and then
let $R\to\infty$. It gives the first convergence locally uniformly;
Cauchy's formula gives all derivatives. The inverse maps have the
same property, and their domains also exhaust $\C$. For a smooth
compactly supported test function $g$,
\[
 (h\circ\psi_n^{-1},g)
       =(h,|\psi_n'|^2(g\circ\psi_n)).
\]
The test functions on the right converge with all derivatives and
have a common compact support. This proves \eqref{eq:field-as}.
Approximating $h$ locally in $H^{-s}$ by smooth functions, and
using the uniform local bounds for pullback by these diffeomorphisms,
also gives convergence in $H^{-s}_{\mathrm{loc}}$.

Here are the local Gaussian details for \eqref{eq:field-tv}; they
also explain the use of the smooth normalization. Choose a disk
$B_R$ containing the compact set in question and the support of
$\rho_a$, and a much larger disk $B_L$. A GFF restricted to $B_L$,
viewed modulo constants, has a decomposition into an independent
zero-boundary field and a harmonic field. Recenter both summands
against $\rho_a$. The zero-boundary summand is the same for any
of the fields being compared, while the recentered harmonic
summand has small Dirichlet energy on $B_{2R}$ as $L\to\infty$.

For completeness, this last estimate follows directly from the
disk Green function. For a whole-plane field, the covariance of
the harmonic part after subtracting its value at the center is
\[
 -\log\left|1-\frac{z\overline w}{L^2}\right|,
 \qquad |z|,|w|<L.
\]
On $B_{2R}$ this kernel and its mixed derivatives are
$O_R(L^{-2})$. Integrating against $\rho_a$ gives the same bound
after recentering against $\rho_a$. The mean-square $C^1$ bound
on a smaller disk follows from the harmonic mean-value formula.
For $h\circ\psi_n^{-1}$ the covariance, modulo terms depending
on only one variable, is
$-\log|\psi_n^{-1}(z)-\psi_n^{-1}(w)|$.
On $B_L$, convergence of the maps and their derivatives gives the
same harmonic estimate with an additional $o_n(1)$ term, for
each fixed $L$. One may first carry out the decomposition on a
larger disk and restrict to $B_L$, so every boundary used here
lies strictly inside $V_n$.

Multiply the recentered harmonic part by a smooth cutoff equal
to one on $B_R$ and supported in $B_{2R}$. Its expected Dirichlet
energy is $O_R(L^{-2})+o_n(1)$. A deterministic Cameron--Martin
shift $g$ changes the zero-boundary GFF law by total variation at
most a constant times $\|g\|_\nabla$, by the Gaussian
relative-entropy formula and Pinsker's inequality. Conditional
on the harmonic part, this applies to the independent
zero-boundary field. Integrating the conditional bound and using
Cauchy--Schwarz gives an error
$O_R(L^{-1})+o_n(1)$. Recentring is a measurable map and cannot
increase total variation. Both normalized fields are therefore
within this error of the same normalized zero-boundary field on
$B_L$, restricted to $B_R$. First let $n\to\infty$ and then
$L\to\infty$. This proves \eqref{eq:field-tv}.
\end{proof}

This is the field comparison used in \cite[Lemma 2.4]{GM}, written
with the normalization transported by $w\mapsto aw$. The test
function in the target has scale $|a|$.

We will apply this comparison to measurable metric functions.
The following elementary fact is useful because such functions
need not be continuous.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:lusin}
Let $X_n,X$ take values in a Polish space and be defined on one
probability space. If $X_n\to X$ in probability and their laws
converge in total variation, then $F(X_n)\to F(X)$ in probability
for every measurable map $F$ into a separable metric space.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
By Lusin's theorem, for each $\delta>0$ there is a compact set
$K$ with $\Pp[X\in K]>1-\delta$ on which $F$ is continuous.
Total variation gives $\liminf_n\Pp[X_n\in K]\ge1-\delta$.
On $K$ the function $F$ is uniformly continuous. Convergence in
probability of $X_n$ to $X$ now bounds the failure probability
for the conclusion by $2\delta+o(1)$. Let $\delta\to0$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Confining paths}
For nonnegative lower-semicontinuous functions we use local epigraph
convergence. Thus $f_n\to f$ means that
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*)]
\item $f(x)\le\liminf_n f_n(x_n)$ whenever $x_n\to x$;
\item for every $x$ there are $x_n\to x$ with $f_n(x_n)\to f(x)$.
\end{enumerate}
Here $x$ is a pair of Euclidean endpoints. Closed epigraphs give a
metrizable separable state space; one can use the usual hyperspace
of closed sets on a countable compact exhaustion. We use the
ambient hyperspace, rather than asserting that length metrics
form a closed subspace of it.

There are two simple continuity observations. If $D$ is a
lower-semicontinuous metric on a compact carrier and
$e^{-a_n}D\le D_n\le e^{a_n}D$ with $a_n\to0$, then
$D_n\to D$ in the epigraph topology. The lower bound follows
from lower semicontinuity and the upper bound uses fixed
endpoints. Also, if $f_n\to f$ and $\psi_n,\psi_n^{-1}$ converge
locally uniformly to a homeomorphism and its inverse, then
pullback transports epigraph convergence. For the recovery
condition, pull the recovering endpoints back by $\psi_n^{-1}$.

\begin{lemma}[A confinement event]\label{lem:barrier}
Fix $R<\infty$ and $\delta>0$. There is $S>4R$ such that, with
probability at least $1-\delta$, all whole-plane distances between
points of $B_R$ equal the corresponding distances with paths
confined to $\overline B_S$. The event can be chosen local to
$B_{2S}$ and stable under sufficiently small uniform Weyl
perturbations there.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The distance around $A_{2R,3R}$ is finite almost surely. The
distance across $A_{S/2,S}$ tends to infinity in probability:
by affine covariance it is $S^{\xi Q}e^{\xi h_S(0)}$ times a
positive random variable with a fixed law. Since
$h_S(0)=o(\log S)$ in probability and $Q>0$, the assertion
follows from tightness of the reciprocal of that variable.

Choose $S$ so that, with probability at least $1-\delta$, there
is a separating loop $L\subset A_{2R,3R}$ with
\begin{equation}\label{eq:barrier-margin}
             \len(L;D_h)<\tfrac14
                         D_h(\mathrm{across}\,A_{S/2,S}).
\end{equation}
A path between points of $B_R$ which exits $B_S$ meets $L$
before and after that excursion. Replace the intervening piece
by an arc of $L$. The cost strictly decreases, and the replacement
lies in $B_S$. Taking infima proves the asserted equality,
including the case when no finite path exists.

The loop and crossing tests are local. Under a Weyl perturbation
$g$ with $\|g\|_\infty\le a$, the ratio of their costs changes
by at most $e^{2\xi a}$. The strict margin in
\eqref{eq:barrier-margin} therefore persists for all sufficiently
small $a$.
\end{proof}

\begin{proposition}[The tangent metric]\label{prop:tangent}
Under the hypotheses of Lemma~\ref{lem:field}, with the domains
$U_n$ Greenian, choose
$R_n\to\infty$ with $\overline B_{2R_n}\subset U_n$. Let
\[
 d_n=D_h(\cdot,\cdot;\overline B_{R_n}),\qquad
 e_n=\psi_n^*D_{h^{\psi_n}}^{V_n}
             (\cdot,\cdot;\psi_n(\overline B_{R_n})),
\]
extended by $+\infty$ outside their carriers. Jointly with $h$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:tangent}
                     (d_n,e_n)\longrightarrow(D_h,D_h)
\end{equation}
in probability for local epigraph convergence.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
First fix a compact target disk and a slightly larger neighborhood.
Use Lemma~\ref{lem:domain} to choose one local metric function for
all the normalized field laws in Lemma~\ref{lem:field}, including
the limit law. Its values on a closed disk are lower
semicontinuous compact internal metrics. Lemma~\ref{lem:lusin}
therefore gives convergence in probability of these metrics,
jointly with the original field.

The constant removed in \eqref{eq:field-tv} tends to zero almost
surely, by \eqref{eq:field-as}. The remaining continuous
correction satisfies
\[
 Q\log|(\psi_n^{-1})'|+Q\log|a|\longrightarrow0
\]
uniformly on compact sets. Weyl scaling and the multiplicative
comparison above show that the compact internal target metrics
converge to those for $h^a-Q\log|a|$.

Now fix an inner disk $B_R$. Lemma~\ref{lem:barrier} supplies,
with arbitrarily high probability, a larger deterministic disk
which computes all its distances. Its margin event transfers
to the normalized transformed fields by total variation, and
survives the vanishing Weyl correction. Eventually
$\psi_n(B_{R_n})$ contains this larger target disk. Distances
confined to $\psi_n(\overline B_{R_n})$ therefore agree with the
fixed confined distances on the inner target disk, except on
an event of arbitrarily small probability. The same is true
for the source metrics.

Pull the target convergence back by $\psi_n$. The affine identity
\eqref{eq:affine} identifies its limit with $D_h$. Taking a
countable exhaustion and then letting the confinement error tend
to zero proves \eqref{eq:tangent}. Both coordinates converge in
probability on the original coupling, which also proves the
joint assertion.
\end{proof}

The same conclusion in law applies when $h$ is replaced at step
$n$ by a field $h_n$ with the same normalized GFF law. Indeed,
the joint law at step $n$ is obtained from that one field law
by the same field and metric functions. A positive-weight
mixture supplies common versions for this countable list of
functions. No coupling of the successive $h_n$ is needed.

\section{Recovering prescribed endpoints}\label{sec:endpoints}
Epigraph convergence gives good approximating endpoints, but not
necessarily the endpoints one wants to compare. The next lemma
is the step that replaces Euclidean continuity of the metric.

\begin{lemma}[Endpoint recovery]\label{lem:recovery}
Let $d_n,e_n$ be extended length metrics on sets eventually
containing every compact subset of $\C$, with Euclidean-continuous
finite-length paths. Suppose
$C^{-1}d_n\le e_n\le Cd_n$ and both sequences converge locally
in the epigraph topology to the same metric $D$. Let
$u_n\to u$, $v_n\to v$, where $u\ne v$, and suppose
$L_n=d_n(u_n,v_n)\to L<\infty$.

Assume that for some $M,\theta>0$ and $s_j\downarrow0$, for
$x_n=u_n,v_n$, every fixed $j$, and all sufficiently large $n$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:recovery-bounds}
 \begin{split}
 d_n(x_n,\partial B_{s_j}(x_n))&\le Ms_j^\theta,\\
 d_n(\mathrm{around}\,A_{s_j,\sqrt{s_j}}(x_n))
                                      &\le Ms_j^\theta.
 \end{split}
\end{equation}
Then $\limsup_n e_n(u_n,v_n)\le D(u,v)\le L$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The lower-bound condition gives $D(u,v)\le L$. Choose recovery
endpoints $a_n\to u$, $b_n\to v$ for $e_n$, and paths $Q_n$
between them with lengths tending to $D(u,v)$.

Fix a small outer scale $s_k$. Next choose a smaller scale $s_j$
with $\sqrt{s_j}<s_k/2$. For large $n$, $a_n$ lies inside
$B_{s_j}(u_n)$ and $b_n$ lies outside $B_{s_k}(u_n)$.
Choose a separating loop $\ell_n$ in
$A_{s_j,\sqrt{s_j}}(u_n)$ with $d_n$-length at most
$Ms_j^\theta+o(1)$, and a path from $u_n$ to
$\partial B_{s_k}(u_n)$ of $d_n$-length at most
$Ms_k^\theta+o(1)$, stopped at its first exit. Both this path
and $Q_n$ meet $\ell_n$. By $e_n\le Cd_n$, one can connect
$u_n$ to the intersection of $Q_n$ with $\ell_n$ at $e_n$-cost
at most
\[
                    CM(s_k^\theta+s_j^\theta)+o(1).
\]
Do the same at $v_n$, with disjoint endpoint neighborhoods.
Use the first source-side intersection of $Q_n$ and the last
target-side intersection. Their order is correct since the two
loops separate disjoint endpoint neighborhoods. The middle
portion of $Q_n$ and the two connectors give
\[
 \limsup_n e_n(u_n,v_n)
       \le D(u,v)+2CM(s_k^\theta+s_j^\theta).
\]
Let $j\to\infty$ with $k$ fixed, then let $k\to\infty$.
The use of two scales ensures that the radial connector reaches
the entire loop; its cost is included in the estimate.
\end{proof}

Let $\Delta=\{2^{-j}:j\in\N\}$. Fix $\alpha\in(3/4,1)$ and
$\theta>0$. Call $u\in\partial B_{\alpha r}(z)$,
$v\in\partial B_r(z)$ a regular pair if there is a whole-plane
$D_h$-geodesic between them contained in the closed annulus
$\overline A_{\alpha r,r}(z)$, if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regular-length}
               D_h(u,v)\le(1-\alpha)^{-1}m_{z,r},
\end{equation}
and if, for $x=u,v$ and every $\delta\in\Delta$ with
$\delta\le(1-\alpha)^2$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regular-endpoints}
 \max\{D_h(x,\partial B_{\delta r}(x)),
       D_h(\mathrm{around}\,A_{\delta r,\sqrt\delta r}(x))\}
                         \le\delta^\theta D_h(u,v).
\end{equation}
Only countably many scales are needed for endpoint recovery.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:regular}
For every $K\Subset U$, $\alpha\in(3/4,1)$, $\theta>0$, and
$\eta>0$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regular-prob}
 \sup_{z\in K}\Pp\big[\text{some regular pair at }(z,r)
          \text{ has }e(u,v)>(1+\eta)D_h(u,v)\big]
                       \longrightarrow0\quad(r\to0).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Otherwise choose $r_n\to0$ and $z_n\to z\in K$ with failure
probabilities bounded below. Rescale the field and map by
\[
 \widehat h_n=h(z_n+r_n\,\cdot)
              -(h(z_n+r_n\,\cdot),\rho),\qquad
 \psi_n(w)=\frac{\phi(z_n+r_nw)-\phi(z_n)}{r_n}.
\]
The fields $\widehat h_n$ have the same smoothly normalized GFF
law and $\psi_n'(0)\to\phi'(z)\ne0$. Both metric coordinates
are divided by
$r_n^{\xi Q}\exp\{\xi(h(z_n+r_n\,\cdot),\rho)\}$.
Confine them to expanding closed disks. Proposition~\ref{prop:bilip}
still compares the confined distances. The source geodesic of a
regular pair lies in the unit annulus, so its distance is unchanged
by confinement; a target comparison failure persists because
confinement can only increase the target distance.

The normalized upper bound in \eqref{eq:regular-length} is a
tight log-normal multiple of $(1-\alpha)^{-1}$. Restrict first
to normalized source distances at most $M$. All endpoint positions
then lie in a fixed compact set and their separation is at least
$1-\alpha$. Failure witnesses can be selected measurably after
completion, as explained in Appendix~\ref{app:selection}.

Adjoin the selected endpoints, the source distance, and the
indicator of failure to the joint convergence of
Proposition~\ref{prop:tangent}. The last three coordinates are
tight. Pass to a subsequence and use a Skorokhod representation
in the ambient separable state spaces. The limiting metric
coordinates are equal almost surely. On any represented sample
where failure occurs infinitely often and the source distances
are at most $M$, pass to a further samplewise subsequence with
convergent endpoints and source distances.

For every fixed small dyadic $\delta$, the bounds
\eqref{eq:regular-endpoints} remain valid for the confined
metrics once their carriers contain the corresponding balls.
For point-to-circle distance, stop a near-minimizing path at its
first exit; for around-distance, the loop is already in the small
annulus. Lemma~\ref{lem:recovery} therefore applies and gives
\[
 (1+\eta)L\le\limsup_n e_n(u_n,v_n)
                  \le D(u,v)\le L.
\]
Here $L\ge D(u,v)>0$, since $u\ne v$. This is a contradiction.
Hence the failure probability with the cutoff $M$ tends to zero.
Let $M\to\infty$. Since the argument treats every possible
sequence of centers in $K$, it proves the uniform statement.
\end{proof}

\section{Good annuli along geodesics}\label{sec:annuli}
The comparison in Proposition~\ref{prop:regular} is useful only
if regular segments occupy a definite fraction of a long geodesic.
We now construct the annuli which ensure this. All geometric
estimates in the first two subsections concern the source metric
alone.

\subsection{Small loops near a geodesic}
Put
\[
 \mathcal A=\overline B_{2r}(z)\setminus B_{r/2}(z),\qquad
 \mathcal A_0=\overline B_{3r/2}(z)\setminus B_{3r/4}(z).
\]
A boundary-to-boundary geodesic in $\mathcal A$ is a geodesic for
the metric internal to this closed carrier, with both endpoints
on $\partial\mathcal A$. The endpoints may lie on the same
boundary component.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:loop-ratio}
There is $\theta>0$ such that, with probability tending to one
as $q\to0$, the following holds simultaneously for every
boundary-to-boundary geodesic $P$ in $\mathcal A$, every
$\delta\in\Delta$ with $\delta\le q$, and every
$x\in\mathcal A_0$ for which $P\cap B_{\delta r}(x)\ne\varnothing$:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:loop-ratio}
 D_h(\mathrm{around}\,A_{\delta r,\sqrt\delta r}(x))
       \le\delta^\theta
          D_h(\mathrm{across}\,A_{\delta r,\sqrt\delta r}(x)).
\end{equation}
The probability bound is uniform in $z,r$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
We prove a bound $O(\delta^b)$ for failure at one scale, with
$b>0$, and then sum over dyadic scales. Rescale to $z=0,r=1$;
all ratios are invariant under the resulting random metric
normalization.

Take a lattice of mesh $\delta/100$ covering a fixed neighborhood
of $\mathcal A_0$. Around each lattice point $w$, use annuli
$A_{t,4t}(w)$ with radii separated by a factor of ten, between
$10\delta$ and $\sqrt\delta/10$. There are
$N\ge c\log(1/\delta)-O(1)$ such annuli. By annular tightness,
for a sufficiently large deterministic $M$ each has probability
arbitrarily close to one of satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:one-shell}
 D_h(\mathrm{around}\,A_{2t,3t}(w))
             \le M D_h(\mathrm{across}\,A_{t,4t}(w)).
\end{equation}
Annular iteration, followed by the union bound over
$O(\delta^{-2})$ lattice points, shows that with probability
$1-O(\delta^b)$ at least $N/2$ of these annuli are good at
every lattice point. The iteration exponent can be chosen large
enough to pay for this lattice count.

Fix a geodesic and a point $x$ as in the lemma, and choose
$w$ within $\delta/50$ of $x$. All the test annuli lie between
the two circles centered at $x$, and their surrounding loops
separate $B_\delta(x)$ from the exterior of $B_{\sqrt\delta}(x)$.
They also lie strictly inside $\mathcal A$. List the good
annuli in increasing order of radius. Write $a_j$ for their
crossing costs and choose loops of lengths
$\ell_j\le2M a_j$. Every $a_j$ is positive and finite.

The geodesic enters the innermost ball, while both its endpoints
lie outside all these annuli. Between its first and last hits of
loop $j$, it crosses each smaller test annulus twice. By geodesic
minimality, replacing this portion by an arc of loop $j$ gives
\[
                  2\sum_{i<j}a_i\le\ell_j\le2M a_j.
\]
Consequently, if $S_j=\sum_{i\le j}a_i$, then
$S_j\ge(1+M^{-1})S_{j-1}$. Any path crossing the full annulus
centered at $x$ must cross every test annulus, so its cost is at
least $S_m$, where $m\ge N/2$. The first good loop is an
admissible loop around that full annulus. Hence
\[
 \frac{D_h(\mathrm{around}\,A_{\delta,\sqrt\delta}(x))}
      {D_h(\mathrm{across}\,A_{\delta,\sqrt\delta}(x))}
       \le 2M(1+M^{-1})^{-(m-1)}.
\]
Choose $\theta>0$ smaller than the power of $\delta$ on the
right. For all sufficiently small $\delta$, this is at most
$\delta^\theta$. Finally,
$\sum_{\delta\in\Delta,\,\delta\le q}O(\delta^b)=O(q^b)$.
The construction is simultaneous in the geodesic and its nearby
center, which proves the stated uniformity.
\end{proof}

The ratio on the right of \eqref{eq:loop-ratio} is essential:
the denominator is the crossing distance of the same annulus.
The proof above obtains it by summing the costs of disjoint
crossings and using geodesic minimality at each surrounding loop.

\subsection{Geodesics do not follow a thin circular annulus}
\begin{lemma}\label{lem:thin}
Fix $q>0$. As $\alpha\uparrow1$, with probability tending to one,
every segment of every boundary-to-boundary geodesic in
$\mathcal A$ which is contained in
$\overline A_{\alpha r,r}(z)$ has Euclidean diameter at most
$qr$. The convergence is uniform in $z,r$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Again take $z=0,r=1$. We describe the local obstruction which
prevents a geodesic from following the unit circle. At a fixed
point of that circle and a fixed scale $t$, there is a probability
$p_0>0$, independent of the scale, of finding a loop in
$A_{2t,3t}$ with length less than the crossing distance of
$A_{t,3t/2}$. To see the positive probability, first restrict to
an event where the normalized crossing cost is bounded below
and the normalized loop cost is bounded above. It has positive
probability by annular tightness. Subtract a sufficiently large
smooth bump which is constant on the outer loop annulus and
zero on the inner crossing annulus. Weyl scaling makes the loop
cheaper than the crossing. Cameron--Martin absolute continuity
then gives positive probability for the unshifted field. The
Dirichlet energy of the rescaled bump is constant, so the lower
bound is uniform in scale.

Apply annular iteration to scales between $\eps$ and
$\sqrt\eps/10$. With probability $1-O(\eps^b)$, at least one
such shortcut loop surrounds $B_\eps(x)$, for each fixed point
$x$ on the circle. A boundary-to-boundary geodesic of
$\mathcal A$ cannot enter this ball: its endpoints are outside
the loop, and the segment between its first and last intersections
would cost more than an arc of the loop. All loops used lie in
the interior of $\mathcal A$, so this conclusion also holds for
geodesics whose endpoints lie on its boundary.

Parametrize the unit circle by arclength. Fubini's theorem and
Markov's inequality show that, outside an event of probability
$O(\eps^{b/2})$, the total arclength of points whose
$\eps$-balls can be visited by any such geodesic is at most
$O(\eps^{b/2})$. This statement is simultaneous in the
geodesic, because the shortcut obstruction is deterministic
once its loop exists.

If a connected path lies in $\overline A_{1-\eps/2,1}$ and has
diameter greater than $q$, its radial projection onto the unit
circle covers an arc of length at least $c q$, for small
$\eps$ depending on $q$. Every point of this arc is within
$\eps$ of the path. This contradicts the preceding bound when
$\eps$ is small. Taking $\alpha\ge1-\eps/2$ proves the lemma.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The good event}
Fix the comparison constant $C$ from Proposition~\ref{prop:bilip}
and set $k=(4C^2)^{-1}$. Let
\[
       S=D_h(\mathcal A_0,\partial\mathcal A;\mathcal A).
\]
This barrier cost is positive. Given $p<1$, we may choose a
dyadic $q\in(0,1/100)$, then $\alpha\in(1-q,1)$, and then
$A_0>1$ so that the following four conditions hold jointly with
probability at least $p$, uniformly in $z,r$:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(G\arabic*)}]
\item The conclusion of Lemma~\ref{lem:loop-ratio} holds for all
dyadic $\delta\le q$ and all the specified internal geodesics.
\item The outer loop cost satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G2}
 D_h(\mathrm{around}\,A_{3r/2,2r}(z))
       \le\min\{(1-\alpha)^{-1}m_{z,r},\ kq^{-\theta}S\}.
\end{equation}
\item Every segment of a boundary-to-boundary geodesic in
$\mathcal A$ contained in $\overline A_{\alpha r,r}(z)$ has
diameter at most $qr$.
\item The thin annulus satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G4}
 D_h(\mathrm{around}\,A_{\alpha r,r}(z))
        \le A_0D_h(\mathrm{across}\,A_{\alpha r,r}(z)).
\end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

Here is the order of the choices. Lemma~\ref{lem:loop-ratio}
makes the failure probability of (G1) small when $q$ is small.
The ratio of the outer loop cost to $S$ has a tight law,
independent of $z,r$, so decreasing $q$ also gives the second
bound in (G2). Next choose $\alpha$ sufficiently close to one
for (G3), using Lemma~\ref{lem:thin}, and for the first bound
in (G2), using annular tightness. Finally, for this fixed
$\alpha$, increase $A_0$ to obtain (G4). Assigning a quarter
of the allowed failure probability to each condition proves
the joint assertion.

\Needspace{8\baselineskip}
For $\eta>0$, add one more condition:
\begin{enumerate}[label=\textup{(G\arabic*)},start=5]
\item Whenever a regular pair at $(z,r)$ satisfies
\begin{equation}\label{eq:G5-extra}
 |u-v|\le qr,\qquad
 D_h(\mathrm{around}\,A_{qr,\sqrt q r}(v))\le kS,
\end{equation}
it also satisfies $e(u,v)\le(1+\eta)D_h(u,v)$.
\end{enumerate}
Denote this five-condition event by $F_{z,r}$.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:good}
For every $p<1$ the parameters $q,\alpha,A_0$ can be chosen
independently of $\eta$ so that, for every $K\Subset U$ and
$\eta>0$,
\[
              \inf_{z\in K}\Pp[F_{z,r}]\ge p
\]
for all sufficiently small $r$. The event is determined by the
field on a fixed annular collar of $\mathcal A$, modulo an
additive constant.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Choose (G1)--(G4) with probability strictly larger than $p$.
Proposition~\ref{prop:regular} shows that the additional failure
probability of (G5) tends uniformly to zero. These choices do
not involve $\eta$ until this last scale cutoff.

For locality, only (G5) needs an explanation. Under
\eqref{eq:G5-extra}, there is a loop surrounding both $u,v$ of
source length less than $2kS$. It lies in the middle of
$\mathcal A$. Its target length is less than
$2CkS=S/(2C)$, whereas a target path from this middle region
to $\partial\mathcal A$ costs at least $S/C$. Any excursion
out of $\mathcal A$ can therefore be replaced by an arc of the
loop, for both metrics. Thus both distances between $u,v$ are
computed internally in $\mathcal A$. The same observation
identifies a geodesic specified in (G5) with an internal one.

All the endpoint circles and loops in the regularity tests lie
inside $\mathcal A$. A point-to-circle test uses a path stopped
at its first exit. The five conditions can consequently be
read from the two internal metrics and the circle average on
this annular collar. By domain locality the target internal
metric is a function of $h^\phi$ on the image collar, hence of
$h$ on the source collar. Adding a constant multiplies every
cost and $m_{z,r}$ by the same factor. The event is therefore
invariant under constants. Appendix~\ref{app:selection}
justifies completion of the sigma algebra when endpoint
quantifiers occur.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Good covers]\label{lem:covers}
Fix $K\Subset U$ and $\eta>0$. Almost surely there is a
deterministic sequence $\eps_j\downarrow0$ along which every
$x\in K$ belongs to some $B_{r/2}(z)$ with
$r\in[\eps_j^2,\eps_j]$ and $F_{z,r}$ occurring.
One sequence can be used for a countable compact exhaustion.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Use lattice centers of mesh $\eps^2/100$ in a compact
neighborhood of $K$, and geometrically separated radii between
$\eps^2$ and $\eps$. Choose the probability in
Lemma~\ref{lem:good} sufficiently close to one that annular
iteration gives failure probability $O(\eps^{10})$ at each
center. The union bound over $O_K(\eps^{-4})$ centers gives
failure probability $O_K(\eps^6)$. A center within
$\eps^2/50$ of $x$ has $x\in B_{r/2}(z)$ at every tested
radius. Choose a summable sequence of errors and use
Borel--Cantelli, with a diagonal choice over the exhaustion.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Theorem 1.3}\label{sec:finish}
\subsection{Improving the comparison constant}
We first prove $e\le d$ for a whole-plane GFF restricted to $U$.
Let
\[
 C_* =\inf\{c\ge1:\ \Pp[e(z,w)\le c\,d(z,w)
                         \text{ for all }z,w\in U]=1\}.
\]
Proposition~\ref{prop:bilip} gives $C_*\le C<\infty$.
Intersecting the events for a decreasing sequence of admissible
constants shows that the bound also holds at $C_*$.
Suppose $C_*>1$ and choose $0<\eta<C_*-1$. Keep the
parameters of the good events fixed and put
\begin{equation}\label{eq:new-C}
 C_{\mathrm{new}}=(1+\eta)+
           \frac{A_0}{A_0+1}(C_*-1-\eta).
\end{equation}
Then $C_{\mathrm{new}}<C_*$. We will prove that this smaller
constant also compares the metrics.

\begin{lemma}[An improved geodesic step]\label{lem:step}
Let $P:[0,T]\to U$ be a whole-plane $D_h$-geodesic
parametrized by length. Suppose $F_{z,r}$ occurs, both global
endpoints of $P$ are outside $\overline B_{2r}(z)$, and
$P(a)\in B_{r/2}(z)$. Let $t>a$ be the next exit time from
$B_r(z)$, and let $s$ be the last time in $[a,t]$ at which
$P(s)\in\partial B_{\alpha r}(z)$. Then
\begin{align}
 e(P(s),P(t))&\le(1+\eta)(t-s),\label{eq:new-step}\\
                    s-a&\le A_0(t-s).\label{eq:time-ratio}
\end{align}
Consequently,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:step-total}
       e(P(a),P(t))\le C_{\mathrm{new}}(t-a).
\end{equation}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The segment $P|_{[s,t]}$ lies in the closed thin annulus.
Extend it backwards to the last preceding boundary hit of
$\mathcal A$, and forwards to the next boundary hit. This
gives a boundary-to-boundary internal geodesic to which
(G1)--(G3) apply. The global endpoints being outside
$B_{2r}(z)$ also ensure that $P$ crosses any outer loop before
and after this segment. Geodesic minimality and (G2) yield
\[
                    t-s\le(1-\alpha)^{-1}m_{z,r}.
\]
Condition (G3) bounds the diameter of $P|_{[s,t]}$ by $qr$.

Write $u=P(s)$ and $v=P(t)$. For a dyadic
$\delta\le(1-\alpha)^2$, the segment from $u$ to $v$
crosses $A_{\delta r,\sqrt\delta r}(u)$, since
$|u-v|\ge(1-\alpha)r$. Its crossing cost is at most $t-s$.
Condition (G1) therefore bounds the surrounding-loop cost
by $\delta^\theta(t-s)$. The full geodesic crosses this loop
on both sides of $u$; replacing the intervening piece by the
loop shows that its portion from $u$ to
$\partial B_{\delta r}(u)$ has length at most the same loop
cost. Taking infima proves both bounds in
\eqref{eq:regular-endpoints}. The argument at $v$ is identical.
Thus $(u,v)$ is a regular pair.

Apply (G1) at scale $q$ around $v$. The relevant crossing is
part of the portion of $P$ enclosed by an outer loop. Minimality
and (G2) give
\[
 D_h(\mathrm{around}\,A_{qr,\sqrt q r}(v))
 \le q^\theta D_h(\mathrm{around}\,A_{3r/2,2r}(z))
 \le kS.
\]
The diameter bound supplies the other condition in
\eqref{eq:G5-extra}. Condition (G5) now proves
\eqref{eq:new-step}.

To prove \eqref{eq:time-ratio}, take a near-minimizing loop
around the thin annulus. It meets $P$ before time $a$ and
between $s$ and $t$. The part of $P$ between those hits has
length at least $s-a$ and, by minimality, at most the loop
length. Hence, by (G4),
\[
 s-a\le D_h(\mathrm{around}\,A_{\alpha r,r}(z))
       \le A_0D_h(\mathrm{across}\,A_{\alpha r,r}(z))
       \le A_0(t-s).
\]
All statements with an infimum follow by letting the loop's
length error tend to zero.

The old comparison gives $e(P(a),P(s))\le C_*(s-a)$.
Combine it with \eqref{eq:new-step}, and use
$(s-a)/(t-a)\le A_0/(A_0+1)$. This gives exactly
\eqref{eq:step-total}.
\end{proof}

Now take any finite whole-plane geodesic $P:[0,T]\to U$
with compact image. If $T=0$ there is nothing to prove.
Otherwise $P$ is injective, Euclidean continuous, and a
homeomorphism onto its image. Work on the probability-one
event of good covers for a compact neighborhood of that image.

At a cover scale $\eps$, retain the corridor between the last
exit from $B_{4\eps}(P(0))$ and the first subsequent entry
into $B_{4\eps}(P(T))$. Its endpoint times tend to $0$ and
$T$. Every retained point is at distance at least $4\eps$
from both global endpoints. Therefore every good ball
$B_r(z)$ with $r\le\eps$ containing that point in its inner
half has both global endpoints outside $B_{2r}(z)$, as
required in Lemma~\ref{lem:step}.

Starting at the beginning of this corridor, choose a good ball
and take its next exit time from $B_r(z)$. Repeat from that
time until reaching the end of the corridor. At each complete
step, Lemma~\ref{lem:step} bounds the target cost by
$C_{\mathrm{new}}$ times the source time. There are finitely
many complete steps: the finite covering collection has a
positive minimum crossing cost. If the final chosen ball
contains the rest of the corridor, leave that last partial
step unimproved. Its image has diameter at most $2\eps$,
so its time length tends to zero by uniform continuity of
the inverse of $P$.

The initial and terminal discarded portions also have time
length tending to zero. The old constant $C_*$ bounds their
target costs. Summing the complete steps and sending
$\eps\to0$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:geodesic-improved}
                  e(P(0),P(T))\le C_{\mathrm{new}}T.
\end{equation}
The good-cover event was independent of $P$, so this conclusion
holds simultaneously for every such geodesic.

Finally let $Q$ be any finite-length source path in $U$.
Choose a compact neighborhood $K\Subset U$ of its image.
The source distance from the image to $\partial K$ is positive.
Partition $Q$ into pieces shorter than this distance, and
replace each piece by a whole-plane geodesic between its
endpoints. Each replacement stays in $K$, since leaving $K$
would already cost more than the piece it replaces. Apply
\eqref{eq:geodesic-improved} to these replacements, sum, and
infimize over $Q$. This proves $e\le C_{\mathrm{new}}d$ at
every endpoint pair, contradicting the definition of $C_*$.
We conclude that
\begin{equation}\label{eq:one-sided}
                              e\le d.
\end{equation}

\subsection{Other fields and the inverse map}
We transfer \eqref{eq:one-sided} to the domain fields of the
theorem. Cover $U$ by countably many disks
$B\Subset B'\Subset U$. On $B'$ couple the zero-boundary part
of $h$ to that of a whole-plane GFF $H$. On $B$ the difference
$h-H$ is a random continuous function $g$. The corresponding
target difference is $g\circ\phi^{-1}$.

The one-sided comparison for $H$ compares lengths of every
path. Applying the common Weyl weight preserves this
inequality: for a path parametrized by source length, the
target length measure is bounded by the source length measure,
so the inequality persists after integration against the
positive continuous function $e^{\xi g}$. Subdividing into
small pieces proves the same statement without choosing a
particular parametrization. Thus \eqref{eq:one-sided} holds
for the internal metrics on $B$ associated with $h$.
The simultaneous Weyl event permits dependence between $g$
and $H$.

Intersect the events for the countable disk cover. Any
finite-length path in $U$ has a finite subdivision subordinate
to this cover. Summing the local inequalities and infimizing
gives \eqref{eq:one-sided} for the full domain metric of $h$.

Apply this conclusion on $V$ to $h^\phi$ and $\phi^{-1}$.
The transformed field is again a zero-boundary GFF plus a
continuous function. The chain rule gives
\[
 (h^\phi)^{\phi^{-1}}
   =h+Q\log|(\phi^{-1})'\circ\phi|+Q\log|\phi'|=h.
\]
The inverse inequality is $d\le e$. Together with
\eqref{eq:one-sided}, it proves Theorem~\ref{thm:main} on
any extension used to realize the fields. The metric functions
depend only on the original fields, so the identity descends
to their original probability space, as shown in
Appendix~\ref{app:versions}. All inequalities are between
nonnegative extended distances; the argument never subtracts
infinite values and therefore includes singular endpoints.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Corollary~\ref{cor:countable}]
Choose product realizations for the countable list of source
fields and for their transformed fields. Apply the
positive-weight mixture construction in
Lemma~\ref{lem:domain} separately to the two lists. This gives
one pair of measurable functions $\Phi_U,\Phi_V$.
For each index, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} applies with these
versions and gives the equality on its original probability
space. Realization independence on common extensions proves
the final assertion. If the fields share one probability
space, their countably many full-probability events can also
be intersected.
\end{proof}

\section{Measurable versions and endpoint selection}
\subsection{Versions on the original probability space}\label{app:versions}
This appendix supplies the measure-theoretic details used in
the main text. They do not alter the geometric argument.

Equip $\Dscr(U)$ with its standard measurable structure,
generated by test-function evaluations. Compact internal
metrics are encoded by their closed epigraphs. On an exhaustion
$K_n\subset\operatorname{int}K_{n+1}$, a countable sequence of
these encodings determines the domain metric. This gives a
standard Borel coordinate space for the construction, even
when the open-domain internal distance is not itself lower
semicontinuous at the boundary of a confinement set.

For a fixed law of $h$, construct a product realization over
its original space $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\Pp)$ as in
Lemma~\ref{lem:domain}. Locality on a countable cover and the
common-function argument identify every compact metric
coordinate as a measurable function of $h$. Measurable
factorization therefore yields a function $\Phi_U$ with
\[
                  \Phi_U(h)=D_{H|_U+F}^{U}
\]
almost surely on that product realization, simultaneously in
all endpoints. Extend the function arbitrarily off the
full-measure set on which the required metric coordinates
are consistent. Its coordinate evaluations are measurable.

This version works on every measure-preserving extension
$\pi:\Omega'\to\Omega$ carrying a realization of $h\circ\pi$.
Indeed, sample the auxiliary whole-plane coordinate of the
fixed product realization by its conditional kernel given
$\omega=\pi(\omega')$. This produces a common refinement of
the two extensions. The conditional kernel exists because
the auxiliary distribution coordinate is standard Borel;
no regular conditional probability on $\Omega$ itself is
required. On the refinement, pull back the defining identity
for $\Phi_U$ and use realization independence to identify the
two metrics. The identity thus holds almost surely there.

If $E$ is the event that the required identity holds on
$\Omega'$, its preimage under the measure-preserving
projection has probability one. Therefore $E$ itself has
probability one. The same argument descends the final
covariance identity to $\Omega$. This proves both the
original-space and all-realizations clauses of the main
results, and also shows that existence of realizations is
part of the construction, rather than an implicit assumption.

For a countable family of laws, give each member positive
weight and carry out the preceding construction under the
mixture. A set of mixture measure zero has zero measure under
every component. The resulting functions therefore work for
each law separately. This is the only uniformity in field
laws needed in the paper.

\subsection{Selecting a failing pair}\label{app:selection}
The probabilities in Proposition~\ref{prop:regular} include
an existential quantifier over uncountably many endpoints.
Here is a precise way to interpret that quantifier.

Use the underlying fields, their countably many compact metric
coordinates, endpoints in a compact set, and a path in
$C([0,1],K)$ as coordinates in standard Borel spaces. For a
lower-semicontinuous metric and a Euclidean-continuous path,
its length is the supremum of the sums of distances over
partitions with rational times. Indeed, any finite partition
can be approximated by rational ones, and lower
semicontinuity gives the required inequality. Thus length
is a measurable function of these coordinates. A path is a
geodesic precisely when it joins the specified endpoints and
its length equals their distance.

Point-to-circle and around distances can also be included as
measurable coordinates. One way to verify this is to use
paths on compact carriers with prescribed rational bounds
for their metric speed and their Euclidean modulus. These
path families are compact. The length sublevel relation is
closed under uniform path limits and epigraph lower bounds
for the metrics. For a surrounding loop, use a rational
positive collar away from the inner and outer boundaries;
its separation property is preserved under uniform limits
within that collar. Taking countable unions over
the collars, length bounds, and moduli recovers the relevant
infima. Moving centers and radii are added as ordinary
Euclidean coordinates. The local metric-to-Euclidean modulus
ensures that every finite-length test path is covered by
one of these countable families.

Consequently the relation asserting a source geodesic,
\eqref{eq:regular-length}, the countably many tests
\eqref{eq:regular-endpoints}, and the strict comparison
failure is Borel after the path is included. Its projection
to the field and metric coordinates is analytic, hence
universally measurable. The measurable selection theorem
for analytic relations supplies witnessing endpoints after
completion of the probability space. Only these endpoints,
the bounded source distance, and a binary failure mark are
adjoined in the subsequence argument; a path coordinate
need not be made tight.

These observations also apply to the local annular events.
For any one field law, choose measurable versions of their
indicators in the completed local sigma algebra. Only
countably many centers and scales enter a cover, so all
versions can be fixed on one full-probability event before
annular iteration and Borel--Cantelli are applied.
